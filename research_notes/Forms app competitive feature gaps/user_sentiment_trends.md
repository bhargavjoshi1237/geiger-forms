# Cross-Cutting Market Signals for Online Form Builders: User Sentiment, Switching Drivers, AI, Conversion, Trust/Compliance, Market (as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Method note: Reddit (reddit.com) and G2's own pages were blocked to the research tools (HTTP 400/403). So user voice here comes from Trustpilot, Capterra, G2 reviews republished on AWS Marketplace, vendor primary sources, and secondary review roundups. Many roundups are written by competing vendors, and they are flagged where that matters. Treat ratings and review counts as snapshots: they differ by source and date.

## 1. User pain points and switching reasons (Typeform, Jotform, Google Forms, Tally, Fillout)

### Takeaway
The main complaint across the category is **metered pricing: response caps that hard-stop forms, plus paywalls on basic features**. Typeform draws the most of it (Trustpilot 2.7/5). Users who leave Typeform over price mostly go to Tally (generous free tier) or Fillout (database-connected workflows). Secondary complaints are free-tier branding, clunky logic-building UIs, billing/auto-renewal practices, support quality, and Google Forms' ceiling (branding, logic, file uploads, analysis).

### Cited Findings
**Ratings and review volume (snapshot; varies by source)**
- G2 online-form-builder category page (Oct 2026 snapshot via search): Jotform 4.6/5 (5,747 reviews), Typeform 4.5/5 (1,026), Tally 4.9/5 (162) — [G2 category](https://www.g2.com/categories/online-form-builder)
- Jotform 4.7 on G2 (5,559 reviews) and 4.7 on Capterra (2,924) — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/guides/best-ai-form-builders)
- Google Forms is listed at 4.6/5 (48,000+ reviews) on G2 *as part of Google Workspace*, with no standalone G2 profile, and 4.7/5 on Capterra (11,400+). Fillout has about 30 G2 reviews (4.6), and Tally about 40 on G2+Capterra in that snapshot. Source is ClickUp, a competitor — [ClickUp blog](https://clickup.com/blog/microsoft-forms-alternatives/)
- One guide notes an aggregated Tally 4.8 rating that rested on only four reviews and "tells you nothing" — [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-free-form-builders)
- **Typeform on Trustpilot: TrustScore 2.7/5 ("Poor") from 786 reviews (97 in the last 12 months). Distribution: 58% 5-star, 22% 1-star**, which is polarized — [Trustpilot Typeform](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/typeform.com)
- Tally on Trustpilot: 3.8/5 from only 22 reviews (59% 5-star, 27% 1-star). The profile is unclaimed — [Trustpilot Tally](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/tally.so)
- Fillout has only 4 Capterra reviews, so evidence is thin — [Capterra Fillout](https://www.capterra.com/p/10002520/Fillout/reviews/)

**Response caps and metered pricing (Typeform the main target)**
- Typeform's free plan allows 10 responses/month across the whole account. At 100% usage, forms reportedly switch to Private and stop accepting submissions until the next month or an upgrade — [meetergo](https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/typeform-pricing)
- 2026 Typeform tiers are roughly Free / Basic / Plus / Business at about 10 / 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 responses/month. Caps are a single **account-level pool shared across forms**, and there is generally no à-la-carte response top-up — [JetAdmin, Sep 2026](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/typeform-pricing/); [TinyCommand](https://tinycommand.com/blogs/typeform-pricing-explained). Prices conflict across sources (Basic about $25–$39/mo, depending on source and billing), and Typeform reportedly A/B tests pricing.
- Reviewers describe receiving an upgrade prompt **mid-campaign with no warning** when the cap is hit — [SaaS Flags](https://saasflags.com/products/typeform)
- Direct Trustpilot quotes (2026):
  - "they lure you in with the free plan and then they limit you to 10 form responses" (Sep 26, 2026)
  - "There's a 10 response per month limit" (Aug 11, 2026; survey closed unexpectedly)
  - "they charged me for 3 months directly on my card without my consent" (Oct 1, 2026; billing/auto-renewal)
  - "the support is really low quality: all good words but no technical knowledge / investigation" (Sep 17, 2026)
  - Also: basic features such as redirects and email alerts sit behind higher tiers — [Trustpilot Typeform](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/typeform.com)
- G2 reviews of Typeform (republished on AWS Marketplace) say "100 responses ... cost a whopping 30€ per month". Multi-user access requires upgrading, so "it gets quiet expensive" for teams — [AWS Marketplace (G2 reviews)](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-l3so72nhizwnw)
- A vendor-written article frames the problem as: "You're not paying for features. You're paying for the right to keep receiving responses." This is the author's opinion; PollPe is a competitor — [PollPe](https://pollpe.com/blogs/typeform-too-expensive-alternatives)
- A 2026 buyer guide ranks form builders explicitly by "whether the free plan caps responses", a sign that caps are now a primary buyer criterion — [BestFirms](https://www.bestfirms.org/best-form-builder-software-2026/)

**Logic-building usability**
- Typeform G2 reviews: "a little clunky and difficult to set up the logic jumps", "Logic jump structure could be cumbersome", "logical jumps get messy sometimes". Others praise the same logic engine as powerful — [AWS Marketplace (G2 reviews)](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-l3so72nhizwnw)
- Tally Trustpilot complaints describe conditional logic as limited or awkward, and say there are no quiz funnels with multiple endings, no signatures, no single-submission CSV export, and no calendar integration. Quote: "the product is very complicated and cannot do quiz funnels" (Nov 6, 2025) — [Trustpilot Tally](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/tally.so)

**Jotform: free-tier limits, branding, price jumps, cluttered UI**
- Free Starter tier: 5 active forms, 100 monthly submissions, 100 MB uploads, Jotform branding, and small payment and e-signature allowances (10 payment submissions/month free, 100 on Bronze). Reaching a limit can **suddenly disable forms** unless you upgrade — [SmallBusinessTools](https://www.smallbusinesstools.org/i/jotform); [Capterra Jotform reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/158456/JotForm-4-0/reviews/)
- Branding "visible on all free plans and hard to remove". Self-serve price escalation runs $39→$129/mo, and "paid plans get expensive faster than Tally or Fillout" — [Workyard](https://www.workyard.com/compare/jotform-review); [Canopyne](https://canopyne.com/jotform-review/)
- One Capterra reviewer: "can feel cluttered at times—there are a lot of menus and settings". Capterra cons also include "frequent glitches and slow support" — [Capterra Jotform](https://www.capterra.com/p/158456/JotForm-4-0/reviews/)
- An analysis of 15 *recent* G2/Capterra Jotform reviews averaged 3.1/5, with about 47% likely to recommend. That is far below the headline 4.7, and the small sample may reflect recency — [Hackceleration](https://hackceleration.com/labs/review/jotform)

**Google Forms' ceiling (sources are mostly competitor marketing)**
- File uploads require respondents to have a Google account, which blocks outside applicants. Forms always look like Google Forms. Branching is limited (no branching on open-ended questions). There are no native payment or e-signature fields, analysis is limited, and response storage is bound by Google Sheets limits — [Jotform: Google Forms problems](https://www.jotform.com/google-forms/google-forms-problems/); [EOI Help Scout](https://eoi.helpscoutdocs.com/article/135-google-forms-limitations)

**Switching destinations**
- Trustpilot reviewers say they moved to Tally over Typeform's price: "No brainer. As easy as Typeform but only a fraction of the price." (Mar 4, 2026) — [Trustpilot Tally](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/tally.so)
- Tally's free tier is described as unlimited forms and submissions within fair use — [Tallyfy comparison](https://tallyfy.com/form-builder-comparison/). Fillout's free tier is 1,000 responses/month — [Fillout blog (vendor)](https://www.fillout.com/blog/best-ai-form-builder)
- Roundups recommend Tally as best value and Fillout "when every answer needs to update Airtable, Notion, or another database" — [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-form-builders)
- Fillout complaints center on support and the handling of paid features. One March 2026 Capterra 1-star review cites a glitch that granted, then removed, paid features without notice — [Capterra Fillout](https://www.capterra.com/p/10002520/Fillout/reviews/)

### Inferences
- The most effective differentiator in user sentiment is **never hard-stopping a live form**. Accept overages with a soft cap, a grace period, or a warning instead of silently going Private. Avoid account-wide shared response pools. Geiger Forms is sold inside a suite, so it can make responses effectively unmetered for suite customers and remove the most-hated pricing pattern entirely.
- Basic outcome features (email notifications, redirects, removing branding) should not sit behind paywalls. Users read gating these as a "bait" pattern.
- Logic is the most-cited *usability* pain after pricing. A visual, readable logic editor is a real gap, even at Typeform.
- Billing hygiene (clear renewals, easy refunds) and responsive, technical support affect review scores out of proportion to their cost. Typeform's 22% 1-star share on Trustpilot comes mostly from billing, caps, and support, not from the builder itself.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit quotes (r/nocode, r/SaaS, r/smallbusiness, r/Entrepreneur): reddit.com was blocked to the research tools. Some secondary blogs paraphrase Reddit, but none were verifiable.
- No quantified survey of *why* users switch (for example, % citing price). Evidence is qualitative.
- G2 product pages returned 403, so G2 "likes/dislikes" theme summaries could not be pulled directly.
- One third-party source claims Typeform raised prices in 2026. This could not be verified against Typeform's pricing page.

## 2. What makes a form tool "delightful" or sticky

### Takeaway
The clearest signals of what is sticky are: **simplicity and speed of building** (Tally's doc-style editor is the reference), a **generous free tier with no response cap**, **native sync into the user's existing data home** (Notion, Airtable, Sheets), and respondent-facing polish (Typeform's conversational UX). Tally's bootstrapped growth to $6M ARR with 10 people, mostly by word of mouth and AI-assistant recommendations, is the strongest quantified proof that this combination works.

### Cited Findings
- Tally reports **$6M+ ARR, 2.5M users, a team of 10, bootstrapped**. Monthly revenue was $501K in Sep 2026, up from $422K in Apr 2026 — [Tally blog, Sep 22 2026](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/). Earlier milestones: $2M ARR (Feb 2025), $3M (Jun 2025), $4M (Oct 2025) — [Indie Hackers (Tally)](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/tally-2#update--MHqY8CCAPSbyKesDDYp)
- Tally attributes its value to simplicity and a product "people genuinely love to use", and describes itself as "the simplest way to create forms, for free". Its "Made with Tally" badge on free forms was its original five-year growth loop — [Tally blog](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)
- Tally user praise: "It's sooo easy to use but also very flexible." (Feb 27, 2026). Reviewers also note monthly feature updates and a support response "within the hour" (Feb 25, 2025) — [Trustpilot Tally](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/tally.so)
- Jotform's most praised trait on G2 is accessibility for "those with limited technical skills". Support is called helpful even on free accounts, though reviews are mixed — [Capterra/G2 roundups via Signeasy](https://signeasy.com/blog/business/jotform-reviews)
- Typeform is consistently credited with the best respondent experience, which one roundup describes as "wins on conversational UX and completion rates" — [Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-form-builders); [BestFirms](https://www.bestfirms.org/best-form-builder-software-2026/)
- Fillout reviewers praise depth: 40+ question types, conditional logic, payments, an API and integrations, and payments, scheduling and PDF generation bundled into one editor — [Capterra Fillout](https://www.capterra.com/p/10002520/Fillout/); [Dupple tools: Fillout](https://dupple.com/tools/fillout)
- Native database sync is a key stickiness driver. Tally's Notion integration is free, first-party, and real-time. Fillout connects natively to Notion, Airtable and Google Sheets — [Tally help](https://tally.so/help/notion-integration); [Paperform roundup](https://paperform.com/form-builders/best-form-builders-for-notion/)
- Notion's own Forms are described as a database view that writes straight into a database. Design and logic are thin, so "public-facing use cases are still better served by Tally or Typeform" — [FreeSurveyMakers](https://freesurveymakers.com/notion-forms/)

### Inferences
- For Geiger Forms, the strongest "sticky" lever available to a suite product is **zero-config, two-way linkage to suite data** (Flow, Notes and other Geiger apps), the way Tally/Fillout plus Notion/Airtable works but native. Notion Forms shows that a native-but-thin form is not enough for public-facing use, so design and logic quality still matter.
- A Tally-style, keyboard-first document editor (type to add blocks, `/` commands) is the build-speed bar users compare against. A badge on free forms ("Made with Geiger Forms") is a proven growth loop, but only if paying users can remove it.
- Respondent polish (Typeform) and builder speed (Tally) are distinct sources of delight. The market rewards having both at a price that isn't metered per response.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes were found specifically about keyboard shortcuts or the "/ command" doc-style editing. That attribution to Tally is widely repeated but not quantified.
- No retention or churn benchmarks for form builders were found.

## 3. AI in form builders (2025–2026): what's adopted vs. gimmick

### Takeaway
**AI form generation (prompt, doc, or URL to form) has become table stakes and shows real adoption.** Tally reports that 30% of forms created by new accounts use its AI, and SurveyMonkey calls AI creation one of its most-used creation methods. Google and Microsoft now bundle AI drafting into their forms tools. The fastest-growing new vector is **forms exposed to external AI assistants via MCP** (Tally, Typeform). Conversational AI agents (Jotform) have large vendor-reported volume. AI response analysis is being shipped, mostly gated to higher tiers, but no independent adoption data exists. User sentiment: AI gives a good first draft, but logic and wording still need human editing. A vocal minority pushes back on AI being forced into the builder.

### Cited Findings
**Adoption evidence**
- **Tally: 30% of forms created by new accounts use Tally AI. Tally AI calls grew from 85K (April) to 767K (August 2026), and AI plus MCP calls topped 1M in August 2026.** AI is optional and opt-in, and the builder UI doesn't change. "Some users have pushed back against adding AI." Tally says it "isn't becoming an AI form builder" and only removes tedium. Users can build forms through Claude or ChatGPT via Tally's **MCP server** — [Tally blog, Sep 2026](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)
- Tally: **43% of new users discover it through ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity or similar**, the top acquisition channel since 2025 — [Tally blog](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/); [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/tally-2#update--MHqY8CCAPSbyKesDDYp)
- SurveyMonkey says its AI survey-creation flow became "one of the most used survey creation methods" on its platform — [SurveyMonkey 2025](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/future-of-surveys-what-our-platform-says-about-2025/). It claims 260K+ organizations use its AI, with no per-feature breakdown — [SurveyMonkey AI](https://www.surveymonkey.com/product/features/ai/)

**Bundled AI in platform forms**
- Google Forms "Help me create" with Gemini was announced June 2025. It builds a form from a prompt or from referenced Docs, Sheets, Slides or PDFs — [Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/06/help-me-create-gemini-google-forms.html). It requires an eligible Workspace or Google AI plan — [Google Docs Editors Help](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/16346789?hl=en). It cannot generate multi-section forms, and using it on an existing form replaces that form — [PlatoForms](https://www.platoforms.com/blog/google-forms-ai-gemini/). AI quiz generation reportedly rolled out Jul–Aug 2026 (third-party guide) — [TeacherCast](https://teachercast.net/instructional-coaching/gemini-google-forms-guide/)
- Microsoft Forms: Copilot can draft from Word, Excel or PowerPoint files or an existing form link (GA rollout mid/late April 2025; requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license) — [M365 Admin (handsontek)](https://m365admin.handsontek.net/?p=11933). It can now also draft from a prompt, suggest fixes, add basic branching, and help collect responses (reminders, tracking) — [Office Watch 2026](https://office-watch.com/2026/microsoft-forms-copilot/); [M365 Admin](https://m365admin.handsontek.net/?p=11924)

**Conversational AI agents**
- Jotform AI Agents launched Feb 25, 2025 for customer service across channels — [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/the-wire/jotform-announces-ai-agents-to-transform-customer-service/). A March 2026 Jotform release claims **750,000+ AI Agents created, 200M+ agent sessions, and 22M+ messages**, with Jotform at 35M+ users — [PR Newswire (Jotform)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jotform-introduces-conversational-ai-for-building-online-forms-302723493.html). This conflicts with Jotform's own 20-year retrospective, which cites "300,000 AI Agent conversations annually" — [Jotform blog](https://www.jotform.com/blog/20-years-of-jotform-by-the-numbers/). Vendor-reported metrics, so treat with caution.

**Typeform's 2026 AI and workflow push (primary sources)**
- Winter '26: AI Data Enrichment, Contacts & Automations, webhook automations, dynamic reporting dashboards, Typeform AI Memory (a brand and company knowledge base), Stripe/PayPal payments, and form commenting — [Typeform Winter 2026 release](https://www.typeform.com/events-webinars/winter-product-release-2026); [Typeform AI data enrichment](https://www.typeform.com/blog/typeform-launches-ai-data-enrichment-to-improve-lead-conversion)
- Growth Flow (May 14, 2026) is an AI customer-lifecycle product spanning capture, enrichment, nurture and conversion — [Typeform blog](https://www.typeform.com/blog/typeform-drives-a-new-era-of-customer-engagement-with-growth-flow). Research Flow (Sep 2026) offers AI-moderated research — [Typeform](https://www.typeform.com/events-webinars/introducing-research-flow-ai-moderated-research-from-typeform)
- Changelog: AI test-response generator (Jan 2026), text-file-to-form upload and editable AI memory (Aug 2026), and Typeform MCP server/Claude support for applying themes (Sep 2026) — [Typeform Changelog](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/29035269414036-Changelog)
- Typeform's "dynamic AI follow-up" (inserting a clarifying question based on an answer) is reported to require a paid plan — [Fillout roundup (vendor)](https://www.fillout.com/blog/best-ai-form-builder)

**AI response analysis**
- SurveyMonkey launched an AI Analysis Suite (Sep 9, 2025), including chat-based "Analyze with AI" and Thematic Analysis (beta), on **Advantage tier and above only** — [SurveyMonkey newsroom](https://www.surveymonkey.com/newsroom/surveymonkey-launches-ai-analysis-suite-and-design-tools/)
- Industry-wide, 1 in 3 CX professionals used AI to analyze customer feedback — [SurveyMonkey AI workplace stats](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/ai-workplace-statistics/)

**Gimmick and quality signals**
- Roundups agree that every AI builder still requires reviewing logic paths and editing question phrasing before publishing. Jotform's generation is "inconsistent with vague prompts" — [Taskade roundup](https://www.taskade.com/blog/ai-form-builders); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/guides/best-ai-form-builders)
- A critique argues that AI builders only speed up creating *static* forms. They don't fix the underlying problem, because the form still can't "clarify ambiguity, probe vague answers, or route based on nuance". The author sells AI interviews — [Perspective AI](https://getperspective.ai/blog/ai-forms-are-not-form-builders-what-to-use-instead)
- Third-party AI form add-ons on the Google Workspace Marketplace draw "useless" reviews, with blank questions and only partial coverage of source content — [Google Workspace Marketplace listing](https://workspace.google.com/marketplace/app/ai_form_builder/678204444887)
- Fillout's AI imports from PDF or existing Google Forms, rewrites question wording, and generates a theme from an uploaded image. Its free tier includes AI generation and 1,000 responses/month (vendor claim) — [Fillout blog](https://www.fillout.com/blog/best-ai-form-builder)

### Inferences
- **Must-have now:** prompt-, doc- and URL-to-form generation, plus "import my existing form" (Google Forms, PDF, Typeform) as a switching on-ramp. Google and Microsoft bundle this, so it is not a differentiator, but not having it is a gap.
- **Emerging differentiator:** an **MCP server/agent API** so users can create forms and read responses from Claude or ChatGPT. Tally and Typeform both shipped this in 2026. Being *recommended by* AI assistants is now a primary acquisition channel (43% for Tally), so public docs, comparison pages and machine-readable facts pages matter. Jotform even publishes a "facts for AI" page — [Jotform facts-for-AI](https://www.jotform.com/facts-for-ai/).
- Keep AI **opt-in and non-intrusive** in the builder (Tally's stance, and its reported user pushback). AI analysis (summaries and themes over open-text responses) is a plausible upsell, but adoption is unproven. Inside a suite, it could be bundled rather than tier-gated as a differentiator against SurveyMonkey and Typeform.
- Conversational AI agents (Jotform) belong more to the customer-service and chatbot category. Defer them unless the suite needs that surface.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) data on how often AI analysis or summaries are used, or on whether AI-generated forms convert differently.
- No user-sentiment data specifically on AI spam detection in form builders.
- Fillout's own AI adoption metrics were not found.

## 4. Conversion and completion optimization: what the data says

### Takeaway
The best-quantified, independent-ish benchmark is Zuko: **about 35% of form viewers complete and about 52% of starters complete**, with a clear mobile penalty. **Autofill/prefill shows the largest association with completion: 62% vs 30%.** Claims that one-question-at-a-time doubles completion rest on vendor marketing with no primary report found. Progress bars help only when they move fast first and then slow, and can hurt otherwise. Capturing partial responses is widely sold, but recovery rates (12–30%) are vendor-reported only.

### Cited Findings
**Zuko benchmarks (Jan 2022–Apr 2025; 739 forms, 93M+ views, about 60M starts, about 38M completions)** — [Zuko](https://www.zuko.io/blog/25-conversion-rate-statistics-you-need)
- View-to-completion **34.6%**, starter-to-completion **51.7%** (so about 48% of starters abandon).
- By form type (view→completion / starter→completion): Registration 46.6% / 60.7%, Purchase 40.0% / 54.4%, Application 37.7% / 51.6%, Comparison 28.6% / 46.4%, **Contact 14.5% / 47.4%**.
- By device: desktop 54.48%, tablet 49.31%, **mobile 47.53%** (6.95 points below desktop).
- **Autofill triggered: 62.2% completion vs 30.2% without** (observational, not causal).
- Field-level (May 2023–Apr 2024; 1,362 forms): mean abandonment of password 10.50%, email 6.41%, phone 6.28%, name 5.27%, postcode 4.82%, address 4.32%.
- Shopify checkout (Jul 2025–Mar 2026): 38.5% view-to-completion. Checkout completion was 94% when a discount code applied successfully vs 35% when it failed, so validation errors are very costly.
- Caveat: Zuko customers skew toward optimization-minded organizations.
- The widely quoted "about 55% abandon" figure comes from an earlier snapshot of Zuko's continuously updated dataset. Current totals imply about 59% of viewers don't complete — [CueFully](https://cuefully.com/blog/form-abandonment-statistics)

**One-question-at-a-time vs classic**
- Typeform's help center has reported an average completion rate of 47% (older version) and 57% (newer: desktop 58%, mobile 56%), defined as reaching submit after opening the form — [Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029615911) (page returned 403 to the fetcher; figures are from the search index).
- A widely repeated claim says a Typeform 2024 report on 2.6M forms found 47.3% completion for one-question-at-a-time vs a 21.5% "industry average" — [TinyCommand](https://tinycommand.com/blogs/conversational-forms-vs-traditional-forms-which-is-better-for-your-business). **No primary Typeform report with these figures could be found. Treat as unverified.**
- Formstack-attributed claims (650K+ submissions: multi-step forms complete 25.4% more than single-page forms; mobile forms showing one field at a time convert 34% higher) appear only in secondary vendor blogs — [Niceform](https://www.niceform.ai/blog/conversational-forms-higher-completion-rates); [TinyCommand](https://tinycommand.com/blogs/conversational-forms-vs-traditional-forms-which-is-better-for-your-business). Unverified.
- Practitioner guidance says multi-field single pages still work well for account creation and checkout — [Venture Harbour](https://ventureharbour.com/form-design-best-practices/)
- Note: Zuko's best-converting form type (registration) is typically a classic multi-field layout. Format is confounded with form length and intent.

**Progress bars**
- A meta-analysis of 32 randomized experiments (Villar, Callegaro & Yang, *Social Science Computer Review* 2013) found constant-speed progress indicators did **not** significantly reduce drop-off. **Fast-to-slow indicators reduced drop-off; slow-to-fast increased it.** Where a small incentive was promised, a constant indicator *increased* drop-off — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/where-am-i-a-meta-analysis-of-experiments-on-the-effects-of-progress-indicators-for-web-surveys/). A critique alleges possible miscoding of drop-off rates — [Neoacademic](https://neoacademic.com/2013/11/07/does-gamifying-survey-progress-improve-completion-rate/)
- A separate study found progress indicators raised breakoff, more so when respondents were told the survey would take 20 minutes rather than 8–10 — [SoundRocket](https://soundrocket.com/web-survey-progress-indicators-quality-implications/)

**Partial-response capture**
- Vendors claim recovery of "up to 19%" of lost leads [Claspo](https://claspo.io/blog/partial-submissions-recover-lost-leads/), "20–30% of would-be lost conversions" or 30–50% of eligible partials [Surface](https://withsurface.com/solutions/recover-form-abandonment), and a modeled 15% recovery cutting cost per lead about 18% [Heyflow](https://heyflow.com/blog/capture-partial-leads/). All vendor-reported. Surface notes a partial response is not blanket consent for outreach, which is a GDPR consideration.

### Inferences
- Highest-confidence levers to build: **prefill via URL parameters and hidden fields, plus browser autofill-friendly markup** (correct `autocomplete` attributes); mobile-first rendering (a measurable 7-point gap); inline validation that prevents error loops; and field-level drop-off analytics (Zuko-style "which field loses people"). That last one is a results-view differentiator few builders offer natively.
- Offer **both** layouts (classic and one-at-a-time) instead of betting on one. Evidence that one-at-a-time wins comes from vendors with a commercial stake.
- If progress bars are offered, compute progress weighted toward early speed, or make them optional. A naive linear bar with logic branching can mislead respondents.
- Partial-response capture is a common premium feature. Ship it with explicit consent handling.

### Gaps
- No primary Typeform, Formstack or Jotform 2025–2026 benchmark report could be retrieved. All "conversational doubles completion" figures remain unverified.
- No independent data on prefill (as opposed to autofill) lift, or on save-and-resume.

## 5. Trust and compliance table stakes (SMB vs enterprise)

### Takeaway
For **SMB/EU self-serve**, the expected baseline is a GDPR DPA, consent fields, an **EU data-hosting option**, spam protection (invisible CAPTCHA such as Turnstile plus a honeypot), and basic accessibility. EU hosting is a real buying reason: Tally and EU-default vendors win on it, and Jotform and Typeform gate or limit it. For **enterprise and regulated** buyers: SOC 2 (Type II), HIPAA BAA (always top-tier gated: Jotform Gold/Enterprise, Typeform Enterprise only), SSO, data retention controls, and EU residency. Even SMB-first Tally is pursuing SOC 2 and ISO 27001 by end-2026 after an August 2026 breach, a signal that certifications are moving down-market. Form platforms are also a **phishing vector**, so abuse prevention is a platform trust issue, not just a feature. The EAA has been in force since June 28, 2025. Its legal baseline is WCAG 2.1 AA via EN 301 549. As of mid-2026 no EAA fines had been confirmed, but regulators and courts are active.

### Cited Findings
**HIPAA BAA**
- Jotform signs a BAA on Gold or Enterprise with HIPAA features enabled (Gold about $99/mo billed annually per secondary sources) — [Unnus](https://unnus.com/hipaa/is-jotform-hipaa-compliant/). - Tally: in August 2026 a third-party analytics tool (Metabase) was compromised and account emails and hashed passwords were extracted (no form data, per Tally). Tally expects **ISO 27001 and SOC 2 by end-2026** — [Tally blog](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)

**Accessibility (EAA / WCAG)**
- The EAA applies to new products and services from **June 28, 2025**. Existing services have until June 28, 2030. The presumption-of-conformity standard EN 301 549 v3.2.1 incorporates **WCAG 2.1 AA**. A revision expected to bring WCAG 2.2 AA is planned — [Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/eu-accessibility-requirements-and-eaa-compliance/); [GetWCAG](https://getwcag.com/en/what-is-european-accessibility-act-eaa). A vendor blog claims forms must meet WCAG 2.2 AA now, which contradicts the standards-based sources — [nForms](https://nforms.eu/blog/eaa-accessible-forms-2025)
- Enforcement status (mid-2026): no confirmed EAA fines under any national law. Sweden's PTS had 28 supervision cases by Mar 3, 2026. The Dutch ACM found **61% of large webshops tested** would not let an assistive-technology user place an order. French disability groups sued four grocers, and a Lille court dismissed the Auchan case on May 5, 2026 (appealed). Italy published its enforcement procedure in May 2026 — [Statutus](https://statutus.ch/en/guides/european-accessibility-act-enforcement-so-far/); [Disability World](https://www.disabilityworld.org/articles/eaa-first-year-enforcement-report/). Fine amounts by country conflict widely across vendor sites.

**Spam, bots and abuse**
- Jotform offers reCAPTCHA, hCaptcha, its own JotCAPTCHA, and "unique submission" limits by cookie or IP — [Jotform Help](https://www.jotform.com/help/205-how-to-add-a-captcha-field/); [Jotform Answers](https://www.jotform.com/answers/34758651-form-spam-prevention-how-to-stop-bots-from-submitting)
- Human-typed spam passes every CAPTCHA and email validator, so content-level filtering is needed — [LeadSource](https://leadsource.co/blog/jotform-spam-submissions)
- Cloudflare Turnstile plus a honeypot is described as the native default in most modern site builders. One agency moved clients to Turnstile because bots flooded forms despite reCAPTCHA and a honeypot — [Build Better Websites](https://buildbetterwebsites.substack.com/p/stop-using-captcha-heres-how-to-actually); [Fat Beehive](https://www.fatbeehive.com/?p=18591)
- **Form platforms as phishing vectors:** Kaspersky (Jul 2025) reported crypto scams sent via Google Forms notifications that bypass mail filters — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/google-forms-exploited-in-crypto-stealing-scam-heres-what-we-know). ESET (Apr 2025) calls Google Forms a popular social-engineering vector — [WeLiveSecurity](https://www.welivesecurity.com/en/scams/how-fraudsters-abuse-google-forms-spread-scams/). NJCCIC (Nov 2025) reported a Docusign-themed credential-theft campaign using compromised Jotform accounts and branding — [NJCCIC](https://www.cyber.nj.gov/Home/Components/News/News/1851/214)

### Inferences
- **SMB table stakes (ship at launch):** DPA and sub-processor list, GDPR consent field plus retention and deletion controls, Turnstile-class invisible bot protection plus honeypot plus rate limiting, WCAG 2.1 AA-conformant respondent rendering (labels, error messaging, keyboard and focus, contrast) targeting 2.2 AA, and clear data-region disclosure. An **EU-hosting option on all plans** would be a differentiator against Jotform and Typeform, which gate or limit it.
- **Enterprise/regulated (roadmap; required for deals):** SOC 2 Type II, SSO/SAML, audit logs, a HIPAA BAA tier, configurable retention, and field-level encryption for sensitive fields. Competitors gate the BAA at the top tier, which creates room to price it lower.
- **Abuse prevention is existential for a suite:** forms hosted on a shared suite domain risk that domain's reputation (blocklisting, mail deliverability) when phishers abuse it. Consider a separate hosting domain for public forms, phishing-keyword and credential-field detection, and report-abuse links. This is an inference from the documented Google Forms and Jotform abuse.
- An accessible respondent experience is now legally relevant in the EU and a selling point to EU SMBs, even though enforcement is early.

- No survey quantifying which compliance items SMB vs enterprise buyers require in procurement. The split above is inferred from how vendors gate features.
- No user-voice data on Tally/Fillout spam problems or account-ban/phishing flagging.
- HubSpot Forms' spam and compliance posture was not researched (no results).

## 6. Market size/growth and competitive dynamics

### Takeaway
Market-size estimates are unreliable (2025 size ranges from $0.58B to $3.8B depending on definition) but consistently show **about 7–15% CAGR**. Three dynamics dominate: (1) **free-tier and pricing wars**, where bootstrapped Tally ($6M ARR, 10 people) and Fillout (1,000 free responses) pressure Typeform's metered model; (2) **bundling of AI-drafted forms into suites** (Google Forms with Gemini, Microsoft Forms with Copilot, Notion Forms as a database view), which commoditizes basic forms; and (3) incumbents **expanding up-stack** into lifecycle and workflow (Typeform Growth Flow and Research Flow, Jotform AI Agents with 35M users). No major 2025–2026 form-builder M&A was found.

### Inferences
- Standalone form builders are being squeezed from below, as suites bundle AI-drafted basic forms, and from a free-tier price war. Incumbents respond by moving up-stack into workflow, CRM-lite, agents and research. For Geiger Forms, the defensible position is the reverse of Notion Forms: **standalone-grade form quality (design, logic, analytics) that is native to suite data and automations**, priced without per-response metering.
- Integration with the suite's own apps (Flow tasks, Notes, a CRM, if one exists) is the moat that standalone players can't replicate cheaply. Fillout and Tally's popularity rests on Notion/Airtable sync, which shows the demand.
- Market-size numbers should not drive decisions. Their spread (6x) shows the category definition is fluid.

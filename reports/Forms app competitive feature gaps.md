# Geiger Forms' Engine Outruns Its Storefront

Geiger Forms already has the back half of a Jotform-class platform. One server-side submission pipeline drives Stripe one-off and subscription checkout, signatures with reviewer countersign, bookable slots, multi-step approvals, field-level drop-off analytics, A/B tests with significance, AES-256-GCM field encryption, retention purges and custom roles, and Geiger gives away free several capabilities that Typeform, Tally, Fillout and Paperform sell as upgrades. What it lacks are the front-of-house features buyers test in their first session, which became table stakes during 2026: AI form generation (**30% of forms created by new Tally accounts already use AI**), one-click importers, custom domains, real design control (Geiger ships three fonts and no CSS), picture-choice and media blocks, native Sheets and Zapier connectors, translated form content, server-rendered PDFs, and an MCP connector at a moment when **43% of Tally's new users find it through AI assistants**. The overall status is **operationally ahead and commercially incomplete**: at par or ahead on collection, response management, funnel analytics, governance and privacy controls; behind on build, design, distribution and ecosystem integrations; and uniquely placed on suite-native fan-out (form to Flow issue today, Events, Dash and Notes next), which no standalone or work-tool form builder offers. The README and UI claim at least four things the code does not do — PayPal, custom domains, CRM integrations and automatic webhook retries — and these should be fixed before any paid beta. Most must-have gaps are small or medium builds on existing plumbing; AI should arrive through the shared `@geiger/ai` package, and several adjacent jobs belong in Flow, Events and Dash rather than in Forms. Throughout, "working" means the code path is complete and persisted, not that it was tested at runtime, and payments, email, captcha and encryption stay dormant until their environment keys are set.

## Rivals now compete on what happens after the submit button

By late 2026 every serious form product covers the whole cycle from build to trust; what separates them is how deep each stage goes and where each is expanding beyond forms. The design-led builders have each picked a lane. Typeform is moving up-market into AI "flows": **Growth Flow costs $266–379 a month** for enrichment, CRM mapping and built-in automations, and Research Flow runs AI-moderated interviews ([Typeform pricing](https://www.typeform.com/pricing); [Typeform blog](https://www.typeform.com/blog/typeform-can-do-what-meet-growth-flow-and-research-flow)). Fillout has become a data platform whose forms pre-fetch and update records in Airtable, Notion, Salesforce and its own Zite database, driven by a Workflows 2.0 engine with loops and branching ([Fillout help index](https://fillout.com/help/llms.txt); [Zite What's New](https://www.zite.com/help/whats-new)). Paperform bundles forms, scheduling, Papersign e-signature and the Stepper automation product into every plan ([Paperform pricing](https://paperform.com/pricing/)); Heyflow sells paid-traffic funnels with A/B tests and server-side Meta CAPI as add-on bundles ([Launch10](https://launch10.com/blog/heyflow-pricing)); and Tally gives away unlimited forms and submissions, payments, signatures, logic and — since July 2026 — AI building ([Tally pricing](https://tally.so/pricing); [Tally changelog](https://tally.so/changelog)). The workflow platforms compete on operations instead. **Jotform is the only vendor that runs intake, approval, document, signature, payment and record natively in one account**, limited by monthly quotas rather than feature gates ([Jotform pricing](https://www.jotform.com/pricing/)); Formstack, now Intellistack, puts approvals, documents and e-sign behind a $250-a-month Suite ([Formstack pricing](https://www.formstack.com/pricing)); Cognito models workflow as roles, actions and statuses on the entry itself ([Cognito workflow guide](https://www.cognitoforms.com/support/408/workflow)); and Zoho ships conditional multi-level approvals plus offline and kiosk collection ([Zoho approval workflow](https://www.zoho.com/forms/approval-workflow.html)).

Beneath them, the free and bundled tools set a floor that keeps rising. Google and Microsoft Forms cover about a dozen question types, section branching, quizzes and a live Sheets or Excel link, and both added generative drafting and response summaries in 2025–26 — but only behind paid Workspace and Copilot licences, and neither collects payments, accepts uploads from anonymous respondents or supports a custom domain ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/search/label/Google%20Forms); [Microsoft 365 Copilot release notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/release-notes); [Jet Admin review](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/google-forms-review/)). Forms inside work tools — Notion, Airtable, ClickUp, monday, HubSpot — share one model, in which the form is a view onto a table and each submission creates exactly one native record, and they share its weaknesses: plan-gated, shallow logic (Notion's conditions are exact-match only and need the Business plan), thin design, almost no analytics, and records that can be created but not updated ([Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/forms); [Airtable Support](https://support.airtable.com/articles/9431794285-building-and-sharing-forms-in-airtable)). The open-source tools are AGPL open-core; OpnForm and HeyForm compete with Typeform on price, while Formbricks is the only form or survey tool that has moved into in-app surveys with SDK triggers, targeting and cooldowns ([Formbricks pricing](https://formbricks.com/pricing); [OpnForm pricing](https://opnform.com/pricing)). At the top, survey and experience-management platforms add a research layer form builders lack: crosstabs with significance tests, quotas and block randomisation, SPSS and PPT exports, surveys in 57 languages, managed contact lists with scheduled reminders, and AI that surfaces findings and explains how it reached them ([SurveyMonkey feature comparison](https://www.surveymonkey.com/pricing/details/?t=0); [SurveyMonkey What's New](https://www.surveymonkey.com/product/features/whats-new/)).

Vendors draw the paywall in the same place. They charge for branding removal, custom domains, partial submissions, drop-off analytics, tracking pixels and custom CSS, and reserve SSO, audit logs, HIPAA and data residency for enterprise tiers ([Tally pricing](https://tally.so/pricing); [Fillout pricing](https://www.fillout.com/pricing); [Typeform pricing](https://www.typeform.com/pricing)). The pricing model users hate most is metering: **Typeform scores 2.7/5 on Trustpilot with 22% one-star reviews**, much of it driven by a free tier that reportedly allows about 10 responses a month across the whole account and makes forms private once the cap is hit ([Trustpilot Typeform](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/typeform.com); [meetergo](https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/typeform-pricing)). Users who leave Typeform over price mostly land on Tally, which has grown to **more than $6M ARR with a team of 10 and no outside funding** ([Tally blog](https://blog.tally.so/6-years-in-6-million-far/)).

Usability depth turns on build speed, respondent polish and logic authoring. Tally's document-style editor sets the build-speed bar and Paperform's works the same way ([tally.so](https://tally.so/); [Paperform product](https://paperform.com/product)); Typeform's one-question-at-a-time flow still gets the credit for respondent polish; and logic authoring is the most-cited usability complaint after price, with Typeform reviewers calling logic jumps "clunky" and Cognito reviewers saying approvals need "complex several-step processes" ([AWS Marketplace G2 reviews](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/reviews/reviews-list/prodview-l3so72nhizwnw); [Software Advice Cognito reviews](https://www.softwareadvice.com/forms-automation/cognito-forms-profile/reviews/)). Vendors are answering with visual logic views — Typeform's Logic Map, Zoho's Rule Flow (July 2026) and SurveyMonkey's logic-visibility panel (June 2026) ([Typeform glossary](https://typeform.com/help/a/a-typeform-glossary-360061281111); [Zoho What's New](https://www.zoho.com/forms/whats-new.html); [SurveyMonkey What's New](https://www.surveymonkey.com/product/features/whats-new/)). Breadth carries its own usability cost: Jotform reviewers say it "can feel cluttered… a lot of menus and settings" ([Capterra Jotform](https://www.capterra.com/p/158456/JotForm-4-0/reviews/)). The best conversion data favours prefill and mobile polish over layout dogma — across 739 forms, **sessions where autofill fired completed 62.2% of the time against 30.2% without it**, and mobile completion trails desktop by about seven points, while the claim that one-question-at-a-time doubles completion traces only to vendor marketing ([Zuko](https://www.zuko.io/blog/25-conversion-rate-statistics-you-need)).

Three release themes defined 2025–26. AI building became universal and agentic, from Fillout Agent and Typeform AI to Tally AI free on all plans, with PDF and image import at Paperform, Tally and Fillout ([Zite What's New](https://www.zite.com/help/whats-new); [Tally changelog](https://tally.so/changelog); [Paperform retro](https://paperform.com/blog/product-retro-2025-2026/)). Forms became addressable from external AI assistants, with Tally, Jotform, Cognito, SurveyMonkey, OpnForm and Heyflow all shipping MCP servers or ChatGPT and Claude connectors ([Jotform newsletters 2026](https://www.jotform.com/newsletters/?year=2026); [Cognito blog](https://www.cognitoforms.com/blog/5-ways-to-use-ai-with-your-form-data); [OpnForm GitHub](https://github.com/OpnForm/OpnForm)). And new field types now complete transactions inside the form — signature, scheduler, payment, video and voice answers, tables and subforms ([Typeform blog](https://www.typeform.com/blog/typeform-can-do-what-meet-growth-flow-and-research-flow); [Fillout help index](https://fillout.com/help/llms.txt)). ## Adjacent specialists charge about $326 a month for post-submit work

Six neighbouring categories show where form builders are expanding, and absorption runs in a clear order. E-signature is the most absorbed: Jotform meters it from 10 to 1,000 signed documents a month, Paperform bundles Papersign into every plan, and Tally gives signatures away ([Jotform pricing](https://www.jotform.com/pricing/); [Paperform pricing](https://paperform.com/pricing/); [Tally help](https://tally.so/help/electronic-signatures)). Scheduling comes next, with Fillout offering routing and round robin free up to 1,000 meetings a month while Typeform still depends on a Calendly block that supports only personal event types ([Fillout Scheduling](https://www.fillout.com/scheduling); [Calendly help](https://calendly.com/help/calendly-typeform)). Lead scoring and routing, then approvals, follow. Client-intake portals with reminder chasers remain largely uncontested — a Jotform support answer from July 2026 confirms there is "currently no option to automatically send reminders to users with incomplete submissions" ([Jotform answers](https://www.jotform.com/answers/40167661-form-submission-reminder-emails-for-incomplete-submissions)) — and in-app feedback widgets and voting boards are the least absorbed of all, with Formbricks the only form tool shipping in-product SDKs ([Formbricks pricing](https://formbricks.com/pricing)). In every category the form handles only capture; specialists charge for what happens next — routing, booking, signing, chasing, approving and notifying.

The prices show how much value sits in those later steps. A three-person team stitching together Calendly Teams, Dropbox Sign, ScoreApp, Content Snare and Canny would pay **about $326 a month at list prices before buying a form builder** ([Calendly pricing](https://calendly.com/pricing); [Dropbox Sign pricing](https://sign.dropbox.com/products/dropbox-sign/pricing); [ScoreApp pricing](https://www.scoreapp.com/pricing/); [Content Snare pricing](https://contentsnare.com/pricing/); [Canny pricing](https://canny.io/pricing)). Bundles inside form builders carry similar premiums: Formstack's Suite (forms, documents, signing and approval routing) costs about **3x** Forms alone, and Typeform's Growth Flow (scoring, routing, enrichment and nurture) costs about **2.9x** Business ([Formstack pricing](https://www.formstack.com/pricing); [Typeform pricing](https://www.typeform.com/pricing/)). Because challengers are already pushing booking and signatures toward free, those now work as parity features; a premium still holds for routing plus enrichment plus nurture, for multi-step approvals with document generation, and for intake chasers. Booking straight after qualification has the strongest, if vendor-published, demand numbers: **a median 62% qualified-to-booked rate across more than a million inbound B2B form submissions** ([RevenueHero](https://www.revenuehero.io/blog/demo-request-to-booked-conversion-rate-benchmarks-in-2026)).

The piece missing across all six categories is a **post-submit state machine**, in which each submission carries a status (awaiting signature, pending approval, missing items, booked) plus timed follow-ups (reminders, escalations, expiry). One such engine can run approvals, chasers and signing reminders instead of three separate features, and Geiger Forms is closer to it than most rivals: it already enforces 8 response statuses in the database, runs a daily cron for reminders, digests and retention, and sends drip follow-ups on a delay ([schema.js](../lib/forms/schema.js); [side-effects.js](../lib/server/side-effects.js)). A simple rule then decides placement — build natively what happens inside the respondent's session (signature, scoring and outcome screens, slot selection, save and resume) and delegate to suite apps what happens after submit, over time or for internal staff. The suite's own roadmap constrains that delegation, because Campaign has been rescoped for later, Comms is folding into Chat and Docs into Dash, and the GA-2027 core is Dash, Notes, Events, Flow, Forms and Chat ([ADD-REMOVE.md](../../ADD-REMOVE.md)). Near-term adjacent work should therefore go to Flow, Events, Dash and Notes plus the shared `@geiger/notify` and `@geiger/ai` packages, and since the category research assumed rather than inspected what each sibling app can do, every delegation target should be confirmed before the team commits to it.

## Geiger Forms is operationally ahead and commercially incomplete

Against that market, Geiger Forms has roughly the breadth of Jotform or Fillout. A single submission pipeline serves both the public filler and REST v1, covering access gates, captcha, rate limiting, validation, stripping of hidden answers, files, bookings, duplicates, signatures, scoring and Stripe checkout ([submit.js](../lib/server/submit.js)), and every post-insert side effect — automations, emails, Slack, webhooks, Flow issues, approvals, Assets sync — runs in isolation so a failing integration never fails a submission ([side-effects.js](../lib/server/side-effects.js)). Its 32 field types beat Google Forms and Tally's free plan and come close to Jotform and Fillout on core types ([field-types.js](../lib/forms/field-types.js)); its logic engine runs identically in the browser and on the server ([logic.js](../lib/forms/logic.js)); and all five migrations are applied, including the RLS lockdown that removes direct anonymous access ([lock_down_rls.sql](../supabase/migrations/20261008155427_lock_down_rls.sql)). Stage by stage, it is ahead on collection, response management, funnel analytics, governance and privacy controls; at par on logic and basic distribution; and behind on build, design, ecosystem integrations and formal certifications. The gaps sit exactly where buyers look first: the four AI views render a Coming Soon screen, there is no importer even though the suite lists a Typeform importer as a priority ([ADD-REMOVE.md](../../ADD-REMOVE.md)), there is no custom domain, design stops at three fonts and a logo entered as a URL, there are no picture-choice or media blocks, translation covers built-in interface text only ([i18n.js](../lib/forms/i18n.js)), there are no native third-party connectors, and PDFs come from the browser's print dialog.

Three caveats limit how far these verdicts can be trusted. The audit read the code but did not run the app; the only automated tests are playground seed tests, so the submit pipeline, logic engine and API are untested ([seed.test.mjs](../lib/playground/__tests__/seed.test.mjs)); and email, Stripe, Turnstile, signed links, encryption and cron all depend on environment keys that, when missing, make a deployment silently skip those features ([email.js](../lib/server/email.js); [stripe.js](../lib/server/stripe.js); [crypto.js](../lib/server/crypto.js)).

A strategic fact hides inside the feature list. Geiger already gives away almost every item on the market's paywall line — branding removal is a free per-form toggle, and partials, drop-off analytics, audit logs and field encryption carry no gate — yet it has no billing code beyond a `forms.billing.manage` permission key, and the paid beta waits on billing in Dash ([geiger-rbac.config.js](../geiger-rbac.config.js); [SALES-PLAYBOOK.md](../../SALES-PLAYBOOK.md)). The strategy doc's "No meter, ever" bet matches the strongest user-sentiment evidence in the category ([geiger-forms-strategy.md](../research/geiger-forms-strategy.md)); the open question is what Geiger will charge for at all. A navigation that doubles as a feature catalogue invites the "cluttered" verdict Jotform gets, and Tally's growth is the counter-evidence that simplicity sells.

## Four false claims and stale docs undercut an otherwise honest product

Most of the product's copy is candid. The HIPAA screen says Forms "is not itself a certified product" ([hipaa_screen.jsx](../components/internal/screens/forms/security/hipaa_screen.jsx)), the AI views say Coming Soon and name `@geiger/ai` ([coming_soon_screen.jsx](../components/internal/screens/forms/coming-soon/coming_soon_screen.jsx)), and per-form sharing is labelled "Advisory" ([access.jsx](../components/internal/screens/forms/forms/sections/access.jsx)) — which is exactly why the exceptions stand out. The webhook claim does the most damage because integrators build on it: with no retries, a single failed POST quietly drops a response from a downstream sheet or CRM ([webhooks.js](../lib/server/webhooks.js)). The README claims bite at the sales gate, where a prospect checking for PayPal or custom domains will find nothing. ## Recommendations sort into three buckets, ranked by evidence

Each recommendation carries a priority and a rough effort. **Must** means that without it Geiger Forms reads as incomplete next to late-2026 rivals or the paid beta is blocked; **Should** means it wins deals or closes a gap competitors already exploit; **Nice** means it differentiates but its absence will not lose a buyer. Effort sizes are my engineering estimates from the codebase audit, not sourced figures: **S** is days to about a week, **M** two to four weeks, **L** one to three months, and **XL** a suite-level effort of a quarter or more. IDs (B1-x, B2-x, B3-x) are used for cross-references.

Worked in order of evidence per unit of effort, the list falls into three phases. The first month is almost entirely S-sized: correct the false claims, then ship webhook retries (B1-6), the auth gate and RLS cleanup (B3-1, B3-2), subject-access export and the consent field (B3-3), the configuration health panel (B3-23), member-linked assignees (B1-8), per-outcome endings (B1-2) and `autocomplete` markup (B1-16). The following quarter's M-sized must-haves close the visible gap — fonts and uploads (B1-3), picture choice and media blocks (B1-1), Sheets and Zapier (B1-5), server PDFs (B1-7), the Typeform and Google importers (B3-21), custom domains (B3-16), the MCP connector (B3-15), tests (B3-22) and the WCAG pass (B3-4). AI generation and analysis (B3-10, B3-11) land as soon as `@geiger/ai` exposes them, while the larger bets — suite record binding (B1-13), the post-submit state machine feeding Flow and Events (B2-1, B2-5, B2-6), and deeper scheduling and e-signature (B2-3, B2-4) — are where Geiger builds what standalone builders cannot copy.

## Conclusion

Geiger Forms has the opposite problem of a typical young form product: the hard, invisible work is done — a server-enforced pipeline, logic that matches on client and server, approvals, payments, encryption, retention and funnels — and what is missing is the cheap, visible layer that buyers judge first and increasingly discover through AI assistants rather than search. On Tally's numbers, an MCP connector, the Typeform and Google importers and AI generation via `@geiger/ai` will do more for adoption than any further operations depth, and the four false claims plus silent environment gating are the cheapest credibility fixes available; left in place, they put an otherwise honest product at risk.

The pricing decision is already half made by what ships free. Geiger gives away the exact features rivals charge for, so the evidence points to charging for AI and automation volume through Dash — the usage meter that Heyflow's AI credits and Paperform's workflow-step allowances are already moving toward ([Paperform pricing](https://paperform.com/pricing/); [Heyflow pricing](https://heyflow.com/pricing/)) — which keeps "No meter, ever" credible on responses and seats. The durable advantage is structural: no standalone or work-tool form was found writing one submission into several apps, and Fillout's record binding shows buyers will pay for forms that act as a front-end to real data. The main risk is depending on suite apps that have not shipped, since Campaign, Comms and Docs are paused or being folded into others; the adjacent roadmap should therefore run through Flow, Events, Dash, Notes and the shared packages, with Forms building the in-session steps itself.
